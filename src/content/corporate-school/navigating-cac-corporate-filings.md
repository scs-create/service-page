Registering a company with Nigeria’s Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) is only the beginning of its corporate compliance obligations. After incorporation, companies must maintain accurate records, document relevant decisions, file required returns and notify the Commission of applicable changes.

Neglecting these responsibilities can result in penalties, inconsistencies in corporate records, delays during transactions and difficulties responding to investor or regulatory due diligence.

A reliable post-incorporation compliance system helps businesses maintain accurate corporate information, meet statutory obligations and prepare for growth.

## 1. Understand Your Post-Incorporation Obligations

Corporate compliance involves more than obtaining a certificate of incorporation. The specific obligations of a business depend on its legal form, size, activities and applicable legislation.

For companies, key areas include:

- Maintaining statutory registers and corporate records.
- Filing annual returns within the applicable statutory period.
- Updating the CAC on reportable changes to company information.
- Recording relevant board and shareholder decisions.
- Maintaining accurate ownership and persons with significant control information, where applicable.
- Complying with relevant sector-specific licensing and regulatory requirements.

The starting point is to review the Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020 (CAMA), the applicable regulations and the company's constitutional documents.

The CAC provides official guidance and downloadable regulatory materials through its [Resources Portal](https://www.cac.gov.ng/resources).

## 2. Establish a Secure Corporate Records Repository

Keep corporate documents in a central, secure repository rather than scattering them across personal devices, email accounts and messaging applications.

The repository should contain, as applicable:

- Certificate of Incorporation and current status report.
- Memorandum and Articles of Association.
- Shareholder and ownership records.
- Director and company secretary information.
- Board and shareholder resolutions.
- Minutes of relevant meetings.
- CAC filing receipts and approved documents.
- Tax registrations, licences and other regulatory records.

Maintain organised electronic copies and retain physical originals where necessary. Restrict access to authorised personnel and establish a backup procedure to prevent accidental loss.

## 3. Maintain Statutory Registers

Statutory registers record important information about a company and its governance. The registers required will depend on the applicable legal requirements.

Relevant records may include:

| Register or record | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Register of members | Records membership and relevant shareholding information |
| Register of directors | Records directors and relevant appointment or cessation details |
| Register of secretaries | Records company secretary information where applicable |
| Persons with significant control records | Identifies reportable persons with significant control in accordance with applicable requirements |
| Register of charges | Records relevant charges created by the company |
| Register of transfers | Helps document share transfers and maintain an accurate ownership history |

Keep these records consistent with the company's constitutional documents, internal records and relevant CAC filings. Where information changes, determine whether an internal update, a formal resolution, a CAC filing or a combination of these actions is required.

## 4. Create a Corporate Compliance Calendar

Missed deadlines are a preventable source of compliance risk. A corporate calendar should identify each obligation, its statutory or regulatory deadline, the responsible person and the evidence required to confirm completion.

Include:

- Annual returns.
- Required board and shareholder meetings.
- Applicable filings arising from changes in directors, shareholders, share capital or registered office.
- Notifications relating to persons with significant control.
- Relevant licence renewals and sector-specific filings.
- Periodic reviews of statutory registers and corporate documents.

Do not assume every obligation follows the same annual schedule. Confirm the applicable deadline and requirements for each filing.

## 5. Document Corporate Decisions Properly

Significant corporate decisions should be documented in accordance with CAMA, the company's Articles of Association and any applicable governance requirements.

Examples may include:

- Appointing or removing directors.
- Approving share allotments or transfers.
- Changing the registered office.
- Altering the company's constitutional documents.
- Approving borrowing or creating relevant charges.
- Authorising major transactions.

Depending on the matter, the appropriate record may be a board resolution, shareholder resolution, meeting minutes or another prescribed document.

Maintain a clear record of the decision, its date, the approving authority and any follow-up filing required. A resolution alone does not replace a statutory filing where one is required.

## 6. Keep Director and Ownership Information Current

Changes in company leadership and ownership must be handled formally rather than relying on informal agreements or internal announcements.

For directors, record relevant appointments, cessations and changes to reportable particulars. For ownership, maintain accurate records of share allotments, transfers and the resulting shareholdings.

Also review whether changes trigger reporting obligations concerning persons with significant control.

Before completing a transaction, confirm that the necessary corporate approvals have been obtained, supporting documents are available and required filings have been completed within the applicable period.

## 7. Understand Annual Returns

Annual returns are statutory corporate filings; they are distinct from tax returns.

They provide prescribed information about a company for the relevant reporting period. Filing requirements, deadlines and applicable exemptions should be assessed under CAMA and the relevant regulations.

The CAC has issued a specific [Annual Return Form for Companies (CAC 19)](https://www.cac.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Annual-return-of-companies.pdf). Companies should consult the current applicable requirements and confirm that the information submitted agrees with their corporate records.

Failure to file required annual returns can attract regulatory consequences. The CAC has expressly warned companies and their directors and officers about penalties for non-compliance in its [public notice on annual returns](https://www.cac.gov.ng/news/5210).

Annual returns should therefore be tracked as a recurring compliance obligation, not postponed until a bank, investor or contracting party requests evidence of good standing.

## 8. Track Post-Incorporation Changes

Changes to corporate information may require formal notification to the CAC. Depending on the circumstances, these may include:

- Change of registered office.
- Appointment or cessation of directors.
- Changes to reportable shareholder information.
- Share allotments, transfers or other changes affecting share capital.
- Changes involving persons with significant control.
- Alteration of the company's name or constitutional documents.
- Registration or discharge of relevant charges.

The applicable filing, supporting documents and deadline depend on the nature of the change. Confirm the current procedure before submission.

The CAC publishes information on available post-registration services through its [official service timelines](https://www.cac.gov.ng/services/service-timelines).

## 9. Use a Filing Checklist and Document-Control Process

A standard checklist helps prevent omissions and inconsistent submissions.

Before making a filing, verify:

- The company's name and registration number.
- The accuracy of the information being reported.
- The required approvals and supporting documents.
- Consistency with existing corporate records.
- The correct filing category and applicable fees.
- The submission deadline.
- The availability of evidence of submission and approval.

Use consistent file names, version numbers and storage locations. Preserve submitted documents, payment evidence, correspondence concerning queries and final approved records.

Where the CAC raises a query, assign responsibility for responding and monitor the matter until it is resolved.

## 10. Assign Compliance Responsibilities

Corporate compliance should have clearly defined ownership, even where the business does not maintain a dedicated compliance department.

| Activity | Suggested responsibility |
|---|---|
| Maintaining corporate records | Designated administrative or legal personnel |
| Monitoring filing deadlines | Assigned compliance coordinator |
| Preparing resolutions and governance records | Company secretary or appropriately qualified adviser, as applicable |
| Approving corporate decisions | Relevant board or shareholders |
| Submitting and monitoring CAC filings | Authorised person or accredited agent, where required |
| Reviewing outstanding obligations | Management or the designated compliance lead |

Delegating administrative tasks does not remove statutory responsibilities imposed on the company, its directors or other relevant persons.

## 11. Conduct Periodic Compliance Reviews

Internal reviews help identify missing documents, overdue filings and inconsistencies before they disrupt a transaction.

A quarterly review may examine:

- The status of annual returns and other required filings.
- Accuracy of director and shareholder records.
- Changes in ownership and persons with significant control.
- Completeness of statutory registers.
- Availability of resolutions and meeting minutes.
- Outstanding CAC queries.
- Upcoming regulatory deadlines.

Record each identified gap, the corrective action required, the responsible person and the target completion date. More frequent reviews may be appropriate for companies undertaking substantial transactions or experiencing frequent changes.

## 12. Prepare for Due Diligence

Investors, lenders, regulators, major clients and commercial partners may request corporate documents before entering into a transaction.

A due diligence file should be capable of providing, as applicable:

- Incorporation documents and a current status report.
- Constitutional documents.
- Current ownership and director information.
- Relevant resolutions and meeting records.
- Evidence of required annual returns and other filings.
- Details of outstanding regulatory issues.
- Relevant licences and sector-specific approvals.

The objective is not merely to produce documents quickly. The records must be accurate, consistent and sufficient for the purpose of the review.

## Practical Example

A real estate company incorporated three years ago is preparing to receive an investment. During preliminary due diligence, management discovers that its shareholder records are outdated, several resolutions cannot be located and its filing history has not been systematically monitored.

The company conducts a compliance review, reconciles its internal records with available CAC records, identifies outstanding obligations and organises its corporate documents. It then assigns responsibility for corrective filings and introduces a compliance calendar.

This process improves the company's ability to respond to due diligence requests and helps management identify issues that require further legal or regulatory attention before the investment proceeds.

## Common Post-Incorporation Mistakes

Businesses should avoid:

- Treating incorporation as the end of corporate compliance.
- Failing to maintain statutory registers and corporate records.
- Recording significant decisions informally.
- Neglecting annual returns.
- Allowing ownership records to become inconsistent with actual transactions.
- Failing to assess whether corporate changes require CAC filings.
- Missing deadlines because no individual is accountable.
- Losing filing receipts, approved documents or query correspondence.
- Assuming CAC filings replace tax or sector-specific regulatory obligations.

## Conclusion

Company registration establishes a formal corporate identity, but sound governance requires ongoing attention to statutory records, corporate decisions and filing obligations.

By maintaining accurate registers, documenting decisions, tracking deadlines and reviewing compliance regularly, businesses can reduce avoidable risks and improve their readiness for investment, financing and commercial transactions.

**Registration establishes the company. Effective post-incorporation compliance helps keep its records accurate, its governance organised and its regulatory obligations under control.**
