## 1. Align Sales Structure with Business Objectives

A sales team should be structured around the organisation's revenue targets, customer segments, products, and growth plans.

Before assigning roles or recruiting additional salespeople, establish:

- Revenue and customer acquisition targets.
- Target markets and customer categories.
- Geographic coverage requirements.
- Sales cycle and expected conversion rates.
- Existing team capacity and future staffing needs.

The structure should reflect the scale and complexity of the business. Smaller organisations may combine several sales functions within one role, while larger teams may require specialised positions.

## 2. Map the Sales Process

Document the stages through which a prospect becomes a customer and identify who is responsible at each stage.

A typical sales process includes:

1. Lead generation.
1. Lead qualification.
1. Customer needs assessment.
1. Proposal preparation and submission.
1. Negotiation and approval.
1. Closing and contract execution.
1. Customer onboarding.
1. Account management and renewal.

Clear ownership at each stage reduces missed follow-ups, duplicated efforts, and opportunities falling between departments.

## 3. Define Sales Roles and Responsibilities

Each position should have a clear purpose, defined responsibilities, measurable expectations, and appropriate decision-making authority.

| Role | Principal responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Sales Director | Sales strategy, revenue planning, major accounts, and departmental performance. |
| Sales Manager | Daily supervision, forecasting, coaching, pipeline reviews, and performance reporting. |
| Business Development Officer | Prospect research, lead generation, initial engagement, and appointment scheduling. |
| Sales Executive | Customer consultations, proposals, follow-ups, negotiations, and closing deals. |
| Account Manager | Customer retention, relationship management, renewals, and account growth. |

These roles are illustrative rather than mandatory. Depending on the organisation's size, one employee may perform multiple functions, provided responsibilities and reporting arrangements remain clear.

## 4. Establish Clear Reporting Lines

Every salesperson should know their immediate supervisor, performance reviewer, and approval authority.

**Illustrative reporting structure**

1. Managing Director
2. Sales Manager
3. Team Leader
4. Sales Representatives

Where a separate sales director or other management layer exists, the reporting structure should reflect the actual organisation.

Document the authority to approve discounts, pricing exceptions, contractual commitments, commission adjustments, and customer escalations. Clear reporting lines reduce conflicting instructions and strengthen accountability.

## 5. Assign Sales Territories and Account Ownership

Sales opportunities should have clearly documented ownership to prevent duplicated outreach and commission disputes.

Ownership may be allocated according to:

- **Geography:** States, cities, or designated regions.
- **Industry:** Construction, healthcare, education, or financial services.
- **Customer size:** Small businesses, medium-sized enterprises, or large corporations.
- **Product or service:** Distinct offerings requiring specialised knowledge.
- **Account assignment:** Named customers or strategic accounts.

Establish rules for lead allocation, reassignment, shared opportunities, inactive prospects, and commission attribution. Record assignments in a central system so that ownership can be verified.

## 6. Document Job Descriptions and Decision-Making Authority

Each sales position should have a written job description covering:

- Job title and purpose.
- Principal duties and expected deliverables.
- Reporting line and working relationships.
- Required qualifications and competencies.
- Performance indicators and targets.
- Authority limits and escalation requirements.

The job description should distinguish between responsibilities the employee can perform independently and decisions requiring managerial approval.

## 7. Establish Meaningful Performance Indicators

Sales performance should be measured against objectives that reflect the organisation's commercial priorities.

| KPI category | Illustrative measures |
|---|---|
| Sales activity | Calls, qualified meetings, and customer follow-ups. |
| Pipeline performance | Qualified opportunities, stage progression, and deal conversion. |
| Revenue | Sales value, gross margin, and revenue against target. |
| Customer acquisition | New customers and acquisition cost. |
| Customer retention | Renewal rates, repeat business, and customer satisfaction. |
| Forecast accuracy | Difference between projected and actual sales. |

Targets should reflect the employee's role, available resources, sales cycle, and level of control over the outcome.

Activity figures alone do not establish effectiveness. They should be assessed alongside conversion, revenue quality, customer outcomes, and profitability.

## 8. Implement a Sales Reporting System

A consistent reporting process allows managers to identify performance problems before they affect monthly or quarterly results.

### Daily reporting

Record significant sales activities, new leads, completed meetings, follow-ups, and immediate obstacles.

### Weekly reviews

Examine pipeline movement, opportunities requiring attention, proposals awaiting responses, expected closures, and agreed next actions.

### Monthly reviews

Evaluate revenue against targets, conversion rates, lost opportunities, forecast accuracy, customer retention, and individual performance.

Reporting requirements should be proportionate to the business. The objective is reliable decision-making, not excessive administration.

## 9. Introduce Sales Pipeline Management and CRM

A sales pipeline provides a structured view of prospective transactions and their progress towards completion.

A typical pipeline may include:

**New Lead → Qualified Prospect → Needs Assessment → Proposal Submitted → Negotiation → Closed Won / Closed Lost**

Each opportunity should contain:

- Customer and contact details.
- Assigned salesperson.
- Current pipeline stage.
- Estimated transaction value.
- Expected closing date.
- Last contact and next action.
- Reasons for delay or loss, where applicable.

A customer relationship management (CRM) system centralises this information, supports follow-up reminders, and improves reporting and forecasting.

The system should be supported by clear data-entry standards, appropriate access controls, and regular record reviews.

## 10. Establish a Fair Commission and Incentive Framework

Sales compensation should encourage behaviour that supports the organisation's commercial objectives.

A documented commission policy should specify:

- Eligible employees and transactions.
- Commission rates and calculation methods.
- Whether incentives are based on revenue, gross margin, or other measures.
- When commissions become payable.
- Treatment of cancellations, refunds, renewals, and shared accounts.
- Rules for lead ownership and disputed transactions.
- Approval, review, and amendment procedures.

The policy should be communicated before the relevant sales activities occur and applied consistently in accordance with employment contracts and applicable law.

Where customer retention and profitability are priorities, the incentive structure should reflect those objectives rather than rewarding new sales volume alone.

## 11. Provide Training and Performance Coaching

Sales performance depends on more than individual talent. Employees need suitable training, management support, and access to the systems required for their roles.

A structured training programme may cover:

- Product and service knowledge.
- Prospect research and qualification.
- Customer communication and needs assessment.
- Proposal writing and presentation.
- Negotiation and objection handling.
- Closing techniques and account management.
- CRM use, reporting, and data accuracy.
- Contractual approval limits and escalation procedures.

Performance reviews should identify specific development needs and establish measurable improvement objectives.

## 12. Establish an Escalation Framework

Sales representatives should understand how to handle issues beyond their authority.

| Level | Typical responsibility |
|---|---|
| Sales Representative | Routine enquiries, standard proposals, and approved follow-ups. |
| Team Leader | Day-to-day coordination, workflow issues, and initial escalations. |
| Sales Manager | Pipeline intervention, performance concerns, and authorised commercial exceptions. |
| Executive Management | Major contractual commitments, exceptional pricing decisions, and significant commercial risks. |

The actual allocation of authority should be documented in the organisation's approval procedures.

## 13. Review and Improve Sales Performance

A sales structure should be reviewed periodically to determine whether it continues to support business objectives.

The review should consider:

- Revenue and profitability against targets.
- Conversion rates and pipeline quality.
- Workload distribution and account coverage.
- Customer complaints and retention.
- Commission disputes and approval delays.
- Training needs and managerial capacity.
- Changes in products, markets, and customer demand.

Adjust roles, territories, reporting arrangements, and performance measures when the evidence supports a change.

## Practical Example: Restructuring a Six-Person Sales Team

A business employs six sales representatives but experiences inconsistent follow-ups, disputed commissions, and unreliable revenue forecasts.

An operational review identifies four weaknesses: unclear lead ownership, inconsistent reporting, the absence of a central customer database, and no documented commission rules.

A structured improvement plan establishes:

- A designated sales supervisor.
- Written role descriptions and reporting responsibilities.
- Documented lead assignment and account ownership.
- A central CRM and standard pipeline stages.
- Consistent performance indicators and review meetings.
- Written commission and escalation policies.

Performance is then monitored against the original problems to determine whether the changes improve lead response, accountability, conversion, and forecast reliability.

The example illustrates how clearly defined responsibilities and processes can address weaknesses that additional recruitment alone may not resolve.

## Common Sales Structure Mistakes

- Recruiting salespeople without defining their responsibilities.
- Allowing multiple representatives to pursue the same prospect without agreed ownership.
- Using unclear reporting and approval arrangements.
- Measuring activity without evaluating results.
- Maintaining inconsistent or incomplete customer records.
- Using ambiguous commission calculations.
- Promoting strong salespeople into management without appropriate support.
- Neglecting coaching, performance reviews, and customer retention.

## Conclusion

An effective sales team requires a structure that connects business objectives with clear responsibilities, reporting lines, performance measures, and reliable sales processes.

When account ownership, decision-making authority, pipeline management, and compensation rules are properly defined, employees can work with greater clarity and managers can make better-informed commercial decisions.

The objective is not simply to increase the number of salespeople, but to build a sales function that is accountable, measurable, and capable of supporting sustainable business growth.
